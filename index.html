About the bags. Within the Kansai region, any hotel front desk will forward your suitcases to the next hotel overnight — the takkyubin courier service, about ¥2,000 a bag — so you travel light on moving days. The little line under each stop shows what happens with your luggage; “forward” means hand it in the evening before, and it's waiting when you arrive.

OSAKA — The trick to travelling well with elders in a Japanese summer is not to see more, but to see it kindly. Late August in Kansai is a furnace of humidity, the thermometer parked near 35°C by noon. So this itinerary bends to the heat rather than defying it: a single outing each morning while the air is still soft, a long midday retreat to a cool room, and an unhurried evening among lanterns and food stalls. Two suitcases travel ahead by courier so no one lifts a bag onto a train. Nobody is ever more than a taxi from shade.

What follows is a week built for four people with different appetites — grandparents who want beauty, warmth and a good meal; a couple chasing a fine vintage watch and a rare handbag — and it turns out those wishes fit together comfortably. The shopping hides indoors during the hottest hours. The hot spring becomes a whole restful day. And the deer of Nara, as ever, do the rest.

The Days, One by One

A dispatch from each morning

Fri · Aug 21

A soft landing

Arrival · Osaka
Stay Swissôtel Nankai Osaka

The flight touches down at Kansai around half past six. After a long travel day, no one should be deciphering ticket machines, so a private car is waiting at arrivals to sweep the family to the Namba hotel in under an hour — bags in the boot, air-conditioning humming.

Dinner stays close and simple: a warm bowl of udon, or an okonomiyaki sizzling on the table griddle a few steps from the door. Then an early night. The real trip begins tomorrow.

Sat · Aug 22

The castle, then the hunt

City day · Osaka
Stay Swissôtel Nankai Osaka

Out early, while it is bearable: Osaka Castle rises from its green moat, and an elevator inside carries you to the view so the grandparents climb nothing. By late morning the sun has teeth, and that is precisely the plan — the afternoon belongs to the covered, air-conditioned arcades of Shinsaibashi and Namba, where the vintage bags and the watches live.

It splits beautifully. The grandparents can nurse an iced coffee while Ping and Nick comb the resale floors; everyone reconvenes as the light turns gold. Evening drifts through Kuromon Market and neon-lit Dotonbori, dinner a plate of crisp kushikatsu skewers or wagyu on the teppan.

See “The Hunt,” below — a full guide to the watch dealers and vintage-bag boutiques, all within a short, cool walk.
Sun · Aug 23

A thousand-year soak

Hot-spring day · Arima Onsen
Stay Swissôtel Nankai Osaka

A direct express bus glides from Osaka to Arima in about an hour — reserved seats, no changes, the gentlest possible outing. Tucked behind the Kobe hills, Arima has soothed travelers for over a millennium, famous for two waters: the iron-rich kinsen, or “gold spring,” a warm reddish-brown, and the clear, carbonated ginsen, its “silver” counterpart.

Soak at the historic Kin no Yu and Gin no Yu bathhouses, or the roomier Taiko-no-Yu spa with its lounging areas. Between baths, wander the sloping lanes for freshly baked carbonated crackers and a slow, warm lunch. A soak, a nap, an early supper — the restorative heart of the week.

Prefer privacy? Many Arima inns rent private baths by the hour — the same spring water, in a quiet room of your own. A dignified, easy option for older travelers.
Mon · Aug 24

Into the old capital

Move day · Kyoto
Getting there Osaka → Kyoto: Shinkansen 14 min from Shin-Osaka (or JR ~30 min), or a door-to-door car ~1 hr
Stay The Thousand Kyoto

The suitcases have already gone ahead by courier, so the family travels to Kyoto with only a day bag — under an hour by train, in reserved seats. Check in, settle, and let the afternoon stay slow.

As the day cools, a first, flat, lantern-lit stroll along the Shirakawa canal and through the lanes of Gion. Dinner is the reward: silky yudofu, simmered tofu served as a graceful set beside a temple garden — warm, delicate, entirely cooked.

Tue · Aug 25

The bowing deer

Half-day trip · Nara
Stay The Thousand Kyoto

A short, reserved-seat ride reaches Nara, and it may become the trip's fondest memory. Hundreds of tame deer roam the broad, flat park, famous for bowing their heads in exchange for a cracker — a small, delighted moment made for grandparents.

A gentle walk leads to Todai-ji, whose vast wooden hall shelters a bronze Buddha nearly fifteen meters tall. Lunch is a soothing iron pot of kamameshi rice before the easy ride home, ahead of the afternoon heat.

An alternative: prefer bamboo to deer? Swap this day for a morning in Arashiyama — the shaded grove, a riverside tofu lunch, and an optional pulled rickshaw so no one tires. There is time for one, not both.
Wed · Aug 26

A slow farewell

Transfer · By the airport
Getting there Kyoto → Kansai Airport: reserved JR Haruka ~80 min, or a door-to-door car ~1 hr 45
Stay Hotel Nikko Kansai Airport

No suitcases today. Because you sent the big bags ahead from Kyoto yesterday, they're already waiting at Hotel Nikko — so you travel with just a small overnight bag and nothing to drag. Enjoy a slow, hands-free morning: The Thousand Kyoto sits right at Kyoto Station, so linger over its food halls and souvenir shops, or take a quick taxi to Nijo Castle and back.

Then an easy run to the airport — the reserved JR Haruka is about 80 minutes from Kyoto Station, or a pre-booked car if you'd rather go fully door-to-door — and you reach Hotel Nikko, joined to the terminal by a covered walkway, to find your luggage already in the room.

The evening is deliberately empty — an early dinner, unhurried packing, a good night's sleep before the flight.

Send the bags Tuesday. Kyoto to the airport area is a next-day delivery, so hand your suitcases to The Thousand's front desk on the morning of the 25th (they'll confirm the day's cutoff) and they'll be waiting on Wednesday. Keep an overnight bag for the one night in between — a change of clothes, toiletries, medicines, and your travel documents.
Thu · Aug 27

Home to Shanghai

Departure · Noon flight

The easiest airport morning imaginable: a calm breakfast, then a five-minute indoor walk from the room to the check-in desks, aiming to be there by half past nine. No train to catch, no weather to face.

A gentle, well-fed, well-soaked week — and the grandparents board the plane rested rather than worn out. That was always the point.

After the Goodbyes · Just Nick & Ping

Two more days, just the two of you

With the grandparents safely homeward, the two of you have two unhurried days before Saturday's late-afternoon flight — and Osaka to yourselves. Nick still wanted mountain air and a big view, so the centrepiece is a day up in Kobe, where a ropeway floats you over herb gardens and a waterfall to sweeping views of the bay, capped with Kobe beef. Around it: an easy return to the city, time for the skincare-and-makeup browse Ping's been eyeing, and a relaxed run to the airport — all close, nothing more than about 45 minutes out.

These three days are the green stretch on the map above — here they are in full.

Thu · Aug 27

Back to the city, at your own pace

Return · Osaka
Getting there Kansai Airport → Namba: Nankai line, about 50 minutes
Stay Hotel Royal Classic Osaka (or your pick)

See your parents through to their noon flight at Kansai, then the two of you ride back into the city on the Nankai line — about fifty minutes to Namba — and check in. With just the two of you now, the pace is entirely your own.

The afternoon is made for the skincare-and-makeup browse through Shinsaibashi (the full guide is just below), an easy wander through Dotonbori as the lights come on, and a relaxed dinner wherever takes your fancy.

A different Osaka hotel. I've pencilled in the Kengo Kuma–designed Hotel Royal Classic at Namba for a change of scene — handy for the airport line on Saturday — but any hotel near Namba or Shinsaibashi keeps you close to the shopping and the food.
Fri · Aug 28

Up Mount Rokko, over Kobe

Mountain & views · Kobe
Getting there Osaka → Shin-Kobe ~30 min, then the Nunobiki ropeway up
Stay Hotel Royal Classic Osaka

A day of mountain air and big views, and all of about 45 minutes out. From Shin-Kobe the Nunobiki Ropeway floats you up the mountainside to the Nunobiki Herb Garden — terraced flowers and greenhouses — with the graceful Nunobiki Falls nearby and the whole of Kobe and Osaka Bay spread out below.

Higher still, Mount Rokko has its Garden Terrace and, as dusk falls, one of Japan's three great night views — the city glittering all the way to the water. Linger for it if you have the energy.

Dinner is the obvious pleasure: Kobe beef, seared on the teppan at a proper Kobe counter, before an easy ride back to Osaka.

Sat · Aug 29

One last Osaka morning

Departure · 4:55 pm flight
Getting there Namba → Kansai Airport: Nankai line, about 50 minutes

No rush. A relaxed breakfast and a final loop of the shops — last skincare picks, any souvenirs you missed, a good coffee — with your bags easily to hand at the hotel.

Then the Nankai line carries you from Namba to Kansai Airport in about fifty minutes; aim to leave the city by roughly 2 pm for comfortable time before the 4:55 pm flight home.

Making the Kobe day easy.  From Osaka, ride to Shin-Kobe (Shinkansen ~13 min, or the subway) and step onto the Nunobiki Ropeway there; a combined Rokko–Nunobiki pass covers the ropeway, the Rokko cable car and the mountaintop buses. Book a Kobe beef dinner a day or two ahead — the best teppanyaki counters fill up — and if you want the famous night view, plan to come down after dusk.
The Hunt · Osaka's Resale District

Watches for Nick, vintage bags for the collection

Japan is among the finest places on earth to buy pre-owned luxury — impeccably kept, meticulously authenticated, often below home prices. In Osaka it clusters in the covered arcades of Shinsaibashi and Namba, cool and dry whatever the sky is doing. Both hunts fit into one afternoon on foot.

Japanese Beauty · Skincare & Makeup

For Ping — the J-beauty haul

Japan is a skincare-and-makeup wonderland, and the best of it sits in the very same Shinsaibashi and Namba arcades as the watches and bags — so it folds neatly into an Osaka afternoon. Two ways to shop it: bright drugstores for cult everyday buys, and the department-store halls for the prestige houses.

Cool Afternoons · Sweets, Coffee & Gifts

Where to hide from the heat — and shop for home

The plan sends everyone indoors during the fierce midday hours, and there is no lovelier way to spend them than over coffee and something sweet in a cool, quiet room. It doubles as gift time: Japan's culture of omiyage — beautifully boxed edible souvenirs — means the department-store food halls and covered markets are a joy for your parents to browse, all air-conditioned. Here are gentle, sit-down-friendly places, chosen so no one is ever standing in a queue in the sun.

Know Before You Go

The comfort brief

Beating the heat

  • Out early, rest at midday, gentle evenings.
  • Water everywhere; hats, folding fans, a cooling towel.
  • Gel patches and battery fans in every drugstore.
  • When in doubt, take the taxi.

At the onsen

  • Rinse and wash before entering; no swimsuits.
  • Tattoos may be restricted — a private bath solves it.
  • Short soaks, water after, never on a full stomach.

Moving about

  • Courier the suitcases hotel-to-hotel (¥2,000–2,500).
  • Reserve every train and the Arima bus seat.
  • An ICOCA tap-card each saves fumbling for tickets.

Health & ease

  • A week's medication, plus spares, in original boxes.
  • One seated rest every 30–45 minutes on foot.
  • Ask the airline for free wheelchair-assist if useful.

Book ahead

  • Arrival car (Aug 21) & airport hotel (Aug 26).
  • The three hotels; the Kyoto splurge fills fast.
  • Arima bus seats; a wagyu and a tofu dinner.

Where you sleep

  • Osaka: Swissôtel Nankai, atop Namba Station.
  • Kyoto: The Thousand Kyoto — serene, right by Kyoto Station.
  • Airport: Hotel Nikko, joined to the terminal.
