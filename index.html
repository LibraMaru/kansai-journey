About the bags. Within the Kansai region, any hotel front desk will forward your suitcases to the next hotel overnight — the takkyubin courier service, about ¥2,000 a bag — so you travel light on moving days. A red note under a stop marks a bag-forwarding day — hand your suitcases in the evening before and they're waiting when you arrive.

The Hunt · Osaka's Resale District

Watches for Nick, vintage bags for the collection

Japan is among the finest places on earth to buy pre-owned luxury — impeccably kept, meticulously authenticated, often below home prices. In Osaka it clusters in the covered arcades of Shinsaibashi and Namba, cool and dry whatever the sky is doing. Both hunts fold neatly into one relaxed morning on foot.

The vintage bags

Hermès · Chanel · Louis Vuitton · Celine
Komehyo Shinsaibashi
The flagship and best first stop — a multi-floor store with thousands of authenticated bags across every brand and price.
Shinsaibashi arcade
Okura
High-end specialist for Hermès Kelly and Birkin, and fine jewelry — the place for a grail piece.
Shinsaibashi
Brand Off
Deep, reliable selection of Louis Vuitton and Hermès with clear condition grading.
Shinsaibashi
Amico Vintage
Boutique for European houses — vintage Celine, Saint Laurent, classic Chanel flaps.
Higashi-Shinsaibashi
Brand Jiku · Vintage Qoo
Collectors' favorites for rarer authenticated Chanel, older LV canvas and Fendi.
Shinsaibashi

The watches

Rolex · Omega · Patek · Seiko · the grails
OKURA Osaka Main Store
Around 2,000 watches with in-house appraisers and watchmakers — the serious first stop for rare and limited models.
3 min from Namba Stn

Specialists in Rolex, Patek Philippe and Audemars Piguet (and designer leather too).
Central Shinsaibashi
Kamekichi
Twenty-five years in the trade, brisk turnover, keen prices, English- and Chinese-speaking staff.
1 min from Shinsaibashi Stn
Best Vintage · Grand Front
Roughly 500 watches from an authorized dealer group, sorted by type — chronographs, divers, military.
Umeda, at Osaka Stn

Broad luxury floor where watches sit beside the bags — handy for a joint browse.
Shinsaibashi
A buyer's field note.  Carry passports for tax-free purchases (usually over ¥5,000); buy from these established, in-house-authenticating names and keep every receipt and guarantee card for the trip home. Prices are fixed, not haggled — and while cards are taken everywhere, a rare find may still prefer cash.
Japanese Beauty · Skincare & Makeup

For Ping — the J-beauty haul

Japan is a skincare-and-makeup wonderland, and the best of it sits in the very same Shinsaibashi and Namba arcades as the watches and bags — so it folds neatly into an Osaka afternoon. Two ways to shop it: bright drugstores for cult everyday buys, and the department-store halls for the prestige houses.

Drugstore cult buys

Affordable, tax-free, gloriously deep shelves
Matsumoto Kiyoshi · Daikoku · Kokumin
The big drugstore chains, one on every Shinsaibashi corner — Hada Labo lotions, Curél, Senka face wash, Melano CC serum, and the famous Anessa & Biore UV sunscreens.
Shinsaibashi · Dotonbori
@cosme STORE
Japan's beauty-ranking retailer — browse the current best-sellers across every brand in one bright shop; superb for discovering what's trending.
Shinsaibashi
Drugstore makeup
The cult labels the world orders online — Canmake, Cezanne, Kate, Excel, Majolica Majorca — at a few hundred yen apiece.
Any of the above

Prestige counters

The high-end Japanese houses, with consultations
Daimaru Shinsaibashi · Takashimaya Namba
Ground-floor beauty halls for the luxe J-brands — Clé de Peau Beauté, Cosme Decorté, SK-II, Suqqu, Three, Addiction, Albion, POLA. Attentive service and gift-wrap.
Shinsaibashi · Namba
Hankyu Beauty (Umeda)
One of Japan's largest cosmetics floors if you want the fullest range — worth the short hop to Umeda for serious browsing.
Umeda
Isetan / department gift sets
Beautifully boxed skincare sets that travel and gift well — ask staff for tax-free at the till.
Depachika counters
Beauty-shopping tips.  Bring your passport — tax-free applies at drugstores too, usually over ¥5,000 in one store. Sunscreens (Anessa, Biore UV) and hydrating lotions are the legendary buys, often far cheaper than at home. Counters will happily let you test shades and match your skin, and most staff can help in English or with a translation app.

Cool Afternoons · Sweets, Coffee & Gifts

Where to hide from the heat — and shop for home

The plan sends everyone indoors during the fierce midday hours, and there is no lovelier way to spend them than over coffee and something sweet in a cool, quiet room. It doubles as gift time: Japan's culture of omiyage — beautifully boxed edible souvenirs — means the department-store food halls and covered markets are a joy for your parents to browse, all air-conditioned. Here are gentle, sit-down-friendly places, chosen so no one is ever standing in a queue in the sun.

Coffee, tea & a sweet

Air-conditioned, unhurried, easy on the knees
Marufuku Coffee
A deep-sofa, Showa-era kissaten — siphon coffee and thick buttered toast in cool, hushed calm.
Osaka · Sennichimae / Shinsaibashi
Rikuro's Cheesecake
The city's beloved wobbly, still-warm cheesecake — a café upstairs, and a boxed one to carry home.
Osaka · Namba
Inoda Coffee
A grand Kyoto coffee house since 1940 — courtyard-quiet, gracious, and thoroughly air-conditioned.
Kyoto · Sanjo (main store)
Kagizen Yoshifusa
A centuries-old wagashi tea room — silky kuzukiri jelly and whisked matcha in a serene tatami salon.
Kyoto · Gion
Nakatanidō
Watch the famously lightning-fast mochi pounding, then eat the warm mugwort mochi on the spot.
Nara · Sanjo-dori
Ask for kakigori
In summer nearly every sweets house serves it — a mountain of fluffy shaved ice with syrup, the ultimate hot-afternoon cure.

At the Border · Arrival Day

Landing at Kansai, step by step

The flight touches down at 6:30 pm on Friday, and the walk from the jet bridge to a waiting car is shorter than it looks — four stops, none of them complicated.

1 · Immigration

  • Join the Foreign Passport queue, Terminal 2.
  • Passport, plus the Visit Japan Web code on each phone.
  • Both index fingerprints and a face photo — quick and routine.
  • Have the hotel address ready if asked where you're staying.

2 · Baggage

  • Carousel boards list 9C6581, in from Shanghai Pudong.
  • 20 kg checked each, plus a 10 kg carry-on.
  • Count every bag before customs — reporting one missing is far easier on that side.

3 · Customs

  • The same code scans again at the electronic gates.
  • No fresh fruit, meat or plants — the rule that catches most people.
  • Cash of ¥1,000,000 or more must be declared.
  • If a gate baulks, the staffed counter takes a paper form instead.

4 · Out the doors

  • Private car to Namba, roughly 50 minutes.
  • Swissôtel Nankai Osaka — 5-1-60 Namba, Chuo-ku.
  • Show a driver the name in Japanese: スイスホテル南海大阪.
Flying home. Ping's parents leave on Thursday 27 August, and the flight departs Kansai at 12:20 — not noon. Two hours for international check-in puts them at Terminal 2 by 10:20, so the car should pull away from Namba around 9:00 am. Their suitcases ride with them: the courier service can't reach the airport the same day.
Know Before You Go

The comfort brief

Beating the heat

  • Out early, rest at midday, gentle evenings.
  • Water everywhere; hats, folding fans, a cooling towel.
  • Gel patches and battery fans in every drugstore.
  • When in doubt, take the taxi.

At the onsen

  • Rinse and wash before entering; no swimsuits.
  • Tattoos may be restricted — a private bath solves it.
  • Short soaks, water after, never on a full stomach.

Moving about

  • Courier the suitcases hotel-to-hotel (¥2,000–2,500).
  • Reserve every train and the Arima bus seat.
  • An ICOCA tap-card each saves fumbling for tickets.

Health & ease

  • A week's medication, plus spares, in original boxes.
  • One seated rest every 30–45 minutes on foot.
  • Ask the airline for free wheelchair-assist if useful.

Book ahead

  • Arrival car (Aug 21); a car to Kansai for your parents on the 27th.
  • The hotels; book early for late August.
  • Arima bus seats; a Kobe beef and a tofu dinner.

Where you sleep

  • Osaka (Aug 21–22): Swissôtel Nankai, atop Namba Station.
  • Arima (Aug 23): Tōsen Goshobō — Japan's oldest ryokan, founded 1191.
  • Kyoto (Aug 24–25): The Thousand Kyoto, by the station.
  • Osaka (Aug 26–28): Hotel Royal Classic, Namba.
